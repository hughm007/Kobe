# Voice of customer — method

**The rule: no invented personas.** Either quote a real customer or mark it UNKNOWN. A persona
assembled from demographic guesses is filler that feels like research, which makes it worse than
an honest gap.

## Where to look, in order of value

| Source | Why it is good | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **The client's own reviews** | Real buyers, this business, recent | Owner-solicited reviews skew positive |
| **Competitor reviews — especially 1–3 star** | Where the market's real pains live | Complaints may be about that operator, not the category |
| **Support tickets / call notes** | Pre-purchase language, objections verbatim | Ask the client; usually not public |
| **Search behaviour** | What they type when the problem is live | Intent ≠ language they'd use aloud |
| **Forums / community threads** | Long-form, unfiltered | May skew to enthusiasts, not typical buyers |
| **Comments on competitor ads** | Reaction to *advertising*, not the service | Loud minority |
| **Testimonials** | Client-approved language | Already polished — closer to marketing than to speech |

For emergency and trades categories, weight **1–3 star competitor reviews** and **live-problem
search language** highest. That is where the fear, the urgency and the real words are.

## What to extract

- **Repeated pains** — the same complaint from different people. Frequency is the signal.
- **Desired outcomes** — what "solved" looks like to them, in their terms.
- **Customer language (verbatim)** — quote it exactly. This is the deliverable.
- **Buying triggers** — the moment they stop researching and act.
- **Fears** — what they are afraid will happen if they choose wrong.
- **High-intent moments** — when the purchase decision is actually made.

## How to record it

Every extraction carries a source and a date:

```markdown
**Repeated pain — CONFIRMED** (source: <where>, <date>)
> "verbatim quote"
Seen in <n> independent reviews.
```

Frequency claims need counting, not impressions. "Several people mentioned" is not evidence;
"7 of 40 reviews mentioned" is.

## The translation rule

When the customer and the brand use different words, **the customer's word wins in ad copy.**

| Customer says | Brand says | Use |
|---|---|---|
| "backed up" | "drainage remediation" | backed up |
| "flooding my kitchen" | "water intrusion event" | flooding my kitchen |
| "how fast can you get here" | "rapid response protocol" | how fast can you get here |

## When there is no evidence

Say so. Write:

> **Voice of customer — UNKNOWN.** Searched: <sources tried>, <date>. No usable customer language
> found. Recommend: <the cheapest way to get it — ask the client for call recordings, read the
> next 20 reviews as they arrive, ask the crew what customers say on the doorstep>.

Then any language used in the ad is labelled HYPOTHESIS and is a testable variable — not an
assumed truth.

## What never happens here

- Inventing a quote, a persona, a demographic or a frequency
- Presenting INFERRED language as customer language
- Writing a testimonial — for a real or synthetic person
  (see `agent-workspace/operations/compliance.md`: never as a customer; fine as an actor)
