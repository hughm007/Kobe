# Personalization tiers — matching research depth to lead value
Single home of the outbound personalization economics. Derived from `gtm-flywheel`
`personalization-engine` (MIT), re-expressed for Service Pow's deal sizes (local-trade
retainers and projects, not enterprise SaaS).

## The trade
Deeper personalization converts better and costs more time. The level is chosen by expected
engagement value, not by enthusiasm:
- **Standard prospects** (typical project/retainer value): variable-driven personalization at
  scale (Tiers 1–2 below) with a strong segment-level angle.
- **High-value prospects** (multi-location operators, flagship-caliber engagements):
  research-derived personalization (Tier 3) written per prospect.
- A well-aimed mid-tier email sent to many qualified prospects beats a hand-crafted one sent
  to a handful — unless the account's value justifies the hours. Say which was chosen and why.

## Variable tiers
| Tier | Source | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — auto-populated | the qualified list itself | name, company, city/metro, trade |
| 2 — enrichment-derived | automated lookups | review count/rating, website status (rubric), GBP activity, service area |
| 3 — research-derived | reading their actual presence | a specific broken thing on their site, a review theme, a competitor comparison observed in their market |

Method: segment the list by (trade × website-status), write one strong body per segment
(Tier 2 angle), layer Tier 1 variables, reserve Tier 3 for the high-value shortlist.

## Discipline
- Keep a running record of which signal types drive positive replies; after a quarter, the
  data — not taste — picks the priority signals.
- Test a no-personalization control periodically: if a strong offer beats weak
  personalization, the copy is doing the work and research time is being wasted.
- Every personalized claim about THEIR business must be true and observed (evidence-labeled);
  a wrong "I noticed…" line is worse than none.
