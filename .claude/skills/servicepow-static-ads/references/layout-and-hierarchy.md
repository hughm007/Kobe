# Layout and hierarchy law — what makes a static readable, then persuasive
The rules the composer enforces and BC-52/BC-53 measure. Taste lives at the critic; floors
live here.

## Hierarchy
- ONE dominant element per ad (the hook — text or image, never both fighting).
- Reading order is designed, not hoped: hook -> support -> proof/brand -> CTA.
- The CTA is a distinct block (chip/button form), never a bare line lost in copy.
- Logo present but never the hero of a direct-response ad; endline position by default.

## Legibility floors (at export resolution, 1080-wide basis; scale proportionally)
- Headline >= 64px · support >= 36px · CTA >= 40px · legal/disclaimer >= 24px.
- Contrast: text vs its actual local background >= 4.5:1 (measured, not assumed — scrims/
  chips exist for busy backgrounds).
- Line length <= ~24 characters for headlines at 1:1/4:5; text blocks never exceed 80% of
  safe-zone width.
- The 350px test: previewed at 350px wide (feed reality), the hook must still read.

## Spacing and alignment
8px base grid · consistent margins per the placement's safe zone · no element closer to an
edge than the safe margin · optical alignment beats mathematical when they conflict, but
the manifest records what was done.

## Copy length budgets (write to fit, don't shrink to fit)
Headline <= 7 words · support <= 14 words · CTA <= 4 words · one idea per ad.

## Distinctiveness (feeds BC-54 and the critic)
Across a delivered set: vary the varying layer visibly (hook text alone is not a variant if
layout, palette and imagery are identical). Against the market: brand palette and real
client assets are the antidote to template-look; "could be any plumber's ad" is a critic
kill, same as video.
