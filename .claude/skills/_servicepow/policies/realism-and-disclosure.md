# POLICY — REALISM AND DISCLOSURE
Always-on company law. The single home of the real-footage rule, the synthetic-people rule, and
AI disclosure. Skills reference this file; they do not restate it. Lineage: reconciles
`38_COMPLIANCE_AND_CLAIMS.md` Rules 1/3 (operator decisions 2026-08-18) with the
human-performance-realism doctrine per Run 2 Phase 4C — one canonical footage rule.

## 1. The footage rule (canonical, replaces all prior variants)
**Use real client media when usable real client media exists for the beat.** The hierarchy for
every shot: real client footage → AI-enhanced real media → pure synthetic.

When usable real media does **not** exist, synthetic content may be used where appropriate —
but it must never falsely present itself as documentary proof that the client performed the
exact depicted work. Concretely:
- A synthetic scenario may **illustrate** the service (an actor illustrates; see §2).
- A synthetic shot may **never** be framed as this client's real jobsite record, real
  before/after, or real customer outcome. Framing decides: captions, VO, and context must not
  assert documentary truth for a synthetic shot.
- Depictions of skilled labour in progress are the highest-risk class: prefer real footage;
  if synthesized, the illustrative (non-documentary) framing above is mandatory and the shot
  carries elevated realism QC.
- Disclosure per §3 applies to every realistic synthetic shot regardless of framing.

## 2. Synthetic people — "never as a customer; fine as an actor"
A generated person may appear the way an actor appears in any commercial: a technician, a
driver, a person in a scenario. A generated person may **NEVER** be presented as a real
customer, reviewer, or endorser. Specifically forbidden: synthetic testimonials or "I used them
and…" statements · a synthetic person as a named/identifiable customer · synthetic UGC framed
as a real unsolicited recommendation · synthetic before/after attributed to a real customer ·
any generated person voicing a star rating, review quote, or satisfaction claim.
**Disclosure does not cure a false endorsement** (FTC Fake Reviews Rule — labelling a fake
customer as AI documents that you knew). The permitted substitute is better anyway: real
footage, a consenting real customer, a real review quoted as on-screen text with attribution,
or a scenario ad. Script-stage test: *would a reasonable viewer believe this person is a real
customer of this business?* If yes and they aren't, the shot is dead at script.
Registry gate: BC-17.

## 3. AI disclosure — always, on every platform
Set the platform AI/synthetic-content toggle on every ad containing realistic generated imagery
of people, places, or events — every time, without a per-ad legal judgement. Over-disclosure is
always legal; non-disclosure risk is asymmetric (label-forcing, removal, account penalties);
platform-safe AI ads are a sellable differentiator. EU-targeting work additionally requires a
clear human-perceivable label at first exposure (EU AI Act Art. 50, in force 2026-08-02).
Never disclose in a way that reads as a confession: the toggle is the mechanism; on-screen copy
says nothing apologetic. Registry gate: BC-18.

## 4. Realism floor
Realism outranks flash. Performed-emotion ban (no celebrations/laughs/claps at readable
distance; payoffs are back-of-head, stillness, small business), reject-on-sight tells (dead
eyes, robotic movement, impossible physics), and measured-motion gates are enforced through the
blocking-check registry (BC-06…BC-15, BC-32, BC-33) and the realism skills. QC claims must
match what was actually verified: state what was not eyeballed, and hold ship until a human
watches it (BC-25).
