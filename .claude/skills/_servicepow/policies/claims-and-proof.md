# POLICY — CLAIMS AND PROOF
Always-on company law. Owner of the evidence standard for every factual, comparative, or
performance claim in any Service Pow deliverable (client ads, the Service Pow website, decks,
proposals). Skills reference this file; they do not restate it.
Lineage: reconciles `38_COMPLIANCE_AND_CLAIMS.md` Rules 2/4/5 (operator decisions 2026-08-18)
with the creative-critic scorecard; the "signed sheet vs email" split is resolved here (§2).

## 1. The claim law
**Any factual or comparative claim requires documented supporting evidence before it enters a
script, a page, or a deliverable.** Not before delivery — before it is written, because a claim
discovered at QC forces a rebuild.

Always require evidence: superlatives ("fastest", "best", "#1", "most trusted") · guarantees of
any kind · specific response times · pricing and savings · licensing, bonding, insurance,
certifications · years in business / jobs completed / customers served · star ratings and review
counts · safety, health, environmental or financial claims · anything comparative about a named
or implied competitor · measurable performance results.

**No documented evidence = the claim does not appear.** Not softened, not hedged — absent.

Preferred creative posture: **demonstrate rather than assert.** "Here is the same-day job,
filmed" outperforms "fastest in town" and needs no substantiation.

Regulated verticals (healthcare, finance, insurance, legal, real estate, employment, credit,
cannabis, gambling, anything involving children) escalate to the APPROVER before production.

## 2. The evidence standard — one bar, one mechanism
The single acceptable bar is a **filed Evidence Record**. A written client confirmation **by
email is sufficient** to create one; a signed sheet is a stronger form of the same record, not a
different standard. No skill may impose a stricter or looser bar than this section.

Every Evidence Record gets an ID and lives in a KB:

```
EV-<subject>-<nnn>
  claim:        the exact wording approved for use
  evidence:     what supports it (email, document, measurement, contract)
  source:       who provided it, and when
  verified:     the date the evidence was last checked against reality
  approver:     the role that signed it off
  scope/expiry: where it may be used; when it must be re-confirmed
  status:       APPROVED | RESTRICTED | WITHDRAWN
```

**Two homes, one structure.** `<subject>` is the client slug for a claim about that client,
and `sp` for a claim Service Pow makes about ITSELF. Claims about Service Pow live in the
Service Pow self-KB and never in a client KB — a client KB holds claims about that client
only. Service Pow's own records are signed off by the APPROVER rather than a CLIENT_APPROVER,
since there is no client to confirm them; everything else about the record is identical.

Deliverables cite Evidence IDs, never re-argue the evidence. A claim without a citable
`EV-` record is an unsubstantiated claim. Registry gate: blocking-check registry, check BC-16.

## 3. Guarantees
A guarantee is a claim (§1) **and** a commitment: it additionally requires APPROVER confirmation
that the business will honor it as written. This applies to Service Pow's own website copy
exactly as it applies to client ads.

## 4. Ad-to-destination parity
Whatever the deliverable promises, the destination must deliver — visibly, above the fold.
Before delivery, actually open the destination and confirm: offer present · price matches ·
guarantee matches · service area matches · CTA possible (phone works, form exists) · not broken
on mobile. Mismatch = the deliverable does not ship, and the mismatch is reported as a finding.
Registry gate: BC-19.

## 5. Rights
Music, stock, third-party footage, likeness, logos, any real person on screen: **online
availability is not permission.** Client-supplied assets are confirmed as theirs to use; real
employees or customers on camera require the client's confirmation that consent exists.
Registry gate: BC-20.

## 6. Client pushback
Held warmly: *"I can't put that in without something behind it — not because I doubt you, but
because it's your account that eats the strike. Send me anything that backs it and it goes
straight in."* A client who insists on an unsupported claim is documented and declined.
