# Compliance and claims

**Ads don't die on motion artifacts. They die on claims, disclosure, and
ad-to-landing-page mismatch.** Five rules; none optional; all block delivery.

## 1 — Synthetic people: "never as a customer; fine as an actor"
A generated person may appear the way an actor appears in any commercial — technician,
driver, fan. A generated person may **NEVER** be presented as a real customer, reviewer
or endorser: no synthetic testimonials, no synthetic UGC framed as a real
recommendation, no generated person voicing a star rating or satisfaction claim.
**The FTC Fake Reviews Rule carries civil penalties, and disclosure does not cure a
false endorsement — labelling a fake customer as AI documents that you knew.**
Script-stage test: *would a reasonable viewer believe this person is a real customer?*
If yes and they aren't, the shot is dead — at script, not at QC.

## 2 — Claims: client substantiates in writing, BEFORE production
Always requiring substantiation: superlatives ("fastest", "best", "#1") · guarantees ·
response times · pricing and savings · licensing/bonding/insurance · years in business
/ jobs completed · star ratings and review counts · safety, health, environmental,
financial claims · anything comparative. Flow: claim listed in the brief → client
confirms in writing (email is fine) → filed under the client's approved claims → used
verbatim. **No written confirmation = the claim is absent. Not softened — absent.**
Preferred posture: demonstrate rather than assert ("here is the same-day job, filmed").
Regulated verticals escalate to Karl before production.

## 3 — AI disclosure: always, on every realistic synthetic ad, every platform
Set the platform AI toggle (YouTube altered-content, TikTok AIGC, Meta AI) every time.
Yes, research says the label can cost some click intent — chosen anyway: the platforms
label automatically now, non-disclosure risk is asymmetric (account-level penalties),
and "platform-safe AI ads" is a sellable differentiator. **EU AI Act Art. 50 is in
force (Aug 2026)** — any EU-targeted work (TripNerd's travel side) needs a clear
human-perceivable label. Never disclose in a way that reads as a confession — the
toggle is the mechanism; on-screen we say nothing apologetic.

## 4 — Ad-to-landing-page parity
The largest single platform-rejection category. Before delivery, open the destination:
offer, price, guarantee, service area all match; the CTA is possible (the number works,
the form exists); the page isn't broken on mobile. A mismatch is reported to the client
as a finding — it's costing them money on every channel.

## 5 — Rights
Music, stock, third-party video, likeness, logos, real people on camera. **Online
availability is not permission.** Client assets are confirmed theirs; on-camera people
need confirmed consent.

## When a client pushes back on a claim
*"I can't put that in without something behind it — not because I doubt you, but because
it's your account that eats the strike. Send me anything that backs it and it goes
straight in."* A client who insists on unsupported claims is telling you what the
relationship will be like.
