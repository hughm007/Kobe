# Production log — "Look Us Up" pack, Pass-1 rounds 1 and 2

All findings below are **CLAUDE-CAUGHT** (the Skeptic gate, run isolated, caught them before a
credit or a shoot hour was spent). Zero OWNER-CAUGHT entries this campaign so far — the ratio
the critic's KPI tracks. Full verbatim attacks: the campaign folder's baseline report appendix
(round 1) and Bible §14 (both rounds).

## Round 1 (7 × S3) — the storyboard as first drafted

1. Generated arrival shot rated EXTREME (two interacting synthetic humans) — restructured.
2. Generated ID badge = fabricated credential, contradicting its own Source field.
3. "Local." — an unregistered claim on an unsigned claims sheet.
4. 24/7 tagline vs a daylight "morning" arrival — the ad contradicted its own tagline.
5. The live ROC record at the heart of the ad had never been eyeballed.
6. S4's footage contingency wasn't gated.
7. AIGC labeling posture undecided on a trust-premise ad.

## Round 2 (3 × S3) — after Revision 1

1. Even ONE static generated figure at 5 ft is the named high-risk class on the trust beat.
2. A standing generation fallback self-marked "NO REFERENCE FOUND — HIGH RISK" is a deferred
   failure, not a fallback.
3. The floor-water dread hook visually depicted a main-line event — the exact banned scope —
   per its own reference citation.

**Resolution: Revision 2 took the board all-practical (zero generation).** Round 2's own
verdict text pointed there: "close to a zero-generation board."

## Round 3 (5 blocking) — the all-practical board

1. The live ROC record check upgraded to a pre-spend BLOCK (advertising-name match is
   regulatory, not cosmetic) — owner-owned.
2. The two queued copy lines are load-bearing comprehension, not garnish — registration is a
   precondition of any variant shooting.
3. C1's live-screen framing collided with S2's own any-frame crop rule (a Revision-2 drafting
   error — the gate caught its author).
4. S4's "preferred" kitchen-sink capture cannot produce the boarded top-down frame — real
   kitchen snaking happens at the trap, not through the basket strainer. The one audience that
   knows is the one the shot exists to convince.
5. The S2 screen composite is professional-grade VFX described in one clause — prove it on a
   test plate or redesign in-camera.

Escalated to the owner after round 3 (the one authorized repair round used). No new copy
violations; the round's clean-checks list grew substantially — the findings are converging on
execution feasibility, which is exactly where a pre-spend gate should end up.

## Reusable lessons (EXPERIMENTAL — promote after repetition)

- **"No client footage" justifies AI only for shots that need the CLIENT.** Brand-free domestic
  actions (a filling sink, a rim overflow, a from-behind pour) are an afternoon of practical
  shooting at zero risk. The board defaulted to "pure AI" where AI was the hardest and least
  necessary route. (Skeptic round 2, structural observation.)
- **A trust ad's synthetic surface is a strategic cost, not just a QC cost** — a platform AI
  label on a verify-before-you-trust ad is a counter-argument, and a competitor screenshot of a
  synthetic technician inverts the concept.
- **Fallbacks are where blocked risk re-enters.** A HIGH-risk path written as "plan B" gets
  exercised at crunch time; the fix is the S4 pattern — any method change re-boards and
  re-gates.
- **Copy claims hide in VO and captions, not just headlines** ("Local.", "Every Arizona
  contractor…", "Verified, at your door." all carried unregistered assertions).
