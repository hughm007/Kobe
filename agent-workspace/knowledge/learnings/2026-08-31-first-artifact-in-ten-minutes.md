# Gates are for publishing, not for drafts

## What happened

The intro film ran a 19-agent adversarial dual gate — the Skeptic's four lenses plus the
creative critic's ServicePow-6 — repeatedly, across sixteen numbered rounds, on drafts.
Each round produced genuine findings. Several were real defects, including one bug present
in every master to date.

The Owner's verdict on the process was still, in his words: *"way too long... I would rather
have something made for me that I can look at within 10 minutes then ask me questions to
make it better together, than take like 15 hours."*

He is right, and the diagnosis is not "the gate is bad." It is that **the gate was answering
a question nobody had asked yet.**

The dual gate exists for work that goes live with money and legal exposure behind it. Its
job is *is this defensible*. On a first draft, the question is *is this the right thing at
all* — and only the Owner can answer that. Running the expensive question first inverted the
loop: hours of verified work accumulated before a single frame was seen by the person whose
opinion decides everything.

The cost compounds. Every gate round hardened a direction that had not been ratified, which
made each subsequent redirection more expensive to absorb.

## The rule

**First artifact inside ~15 minutes, however rough. Then iterate with the Owner. Gate once,
on the frozen artifact, before anything is published.**

Concretely:

1. **Draft stage** — machine QC only, and it should be *cheap and mechanical*: does it hit
   the spec, is any frame blank, is the audio present, does the text fit. These catch the
   errors that waste the Owner's time without spending an hour to run. On this round they
   caught two completely blank frames (standard deviation 0.00, one distinct colour) in
   about forty seconds.
2. **Owner stage** — he watches, he reacts, the direction gets settled.
3. **Publish stage** — now run the full dual gate, once, on the thing that is actually
   going out. This is where "defensible" is the right question.

## The tell

If you are about to run an expensive review on something the Owner has not yet seen, stop.
The review cannot tell you whether he wants it. Show him first.

## Corollary

Machine QC is not a lesser gate — it is a *different* one, and it belongs at the draft
stage precisely because it needs no judgment. Spec conformance, blank-frame sweeps, audio
loudness and per-bucket presence, text legibility at the delivery viewport: all mechanical,
all fast, all worth running on every single build.
