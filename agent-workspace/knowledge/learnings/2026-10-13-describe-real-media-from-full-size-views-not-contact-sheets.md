# Storyboard descriptions written from contact sheets misdescribed real media; cold reviewers caught it

## What we did
I wrote a Reel storyboard (TripNerd Daytona, "Race day, by the clock") from 1-fps contact sheets and 420-px photo thumbnails. Three cold reviewers then checked it against enlarged frames of the same media: the Critic, the Skeptic and a hook tournament, each in a fresh context.

## What happened
They found four errors in my descriptions:
- "Dusk" was full night.
- A "packed grandstand" was empty pre-race seating.
- Two photos I sequenced as a progression were the same moment from the same seat.
- "No faces" was false at the edge of one video window.

Two of these became predicted hard failures. A third error of the same kind was sound written for the wrong picture: a "full-field pass" over a single car's burnout.

**Sample:** one storyboard, 4 errors, all caught before any render.

## What it means
- The 2026-10-07 rule ("never describe a generated clip from a contact sheet") applies equally to real client media.
- Contact sheets are for finding moments, not for describing them.
- Real footage is "proof", so a misdescription is a truth failure, not a cosmetic one.

## What we do differently
1. Describe every planned still and video window from a full-size view before it goes into a storyboard.
2. Look for near-duplicate stills (same seat, same subject) before sequencing them as time passing.
3. Write sound only after checking what the picture physically shows.
4. Keep running cold reviews on paper storyboards, not only on rendered masters. Today they cost about 20 minutes, against a predicted HARD FAIL.
