# The offer line gets the shortest hold in the film — second occurrence

## What happened
| Occurrence | Film | The commercial line | Its treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 911 Drain (08-26 rounds) | the price line | ~242 WPM read — fastest in the ad; caught by check 32's origin case |
| 2 | Intro video challenger (08-30) | `4 ads · 5–7 days` | **1.4s hold, 12.3 cps — the shortest and fastest string in the film**, while "Can't get pulled." held 5.8s |

Rev 3's spine fixed it deliberately: the turnaround claim holds 4.2s and the signed CTA 6.9s — the
**longest** hold in the film, and that superlative was verified, not asserted.

## Mechanism
The commercial line feels "done" to its author (it's the part they know by heart), so it gets
compressed to make room for the persuasion around it — inverting the viewer's priority.

## The rule this earns
The offer/price/CTA string gets the longest or second-longest hold, and that ranking is **checked
by sorting the string table by dwell** before render. Pattern-watch at 2; check 32 already covers
spoken VO — a third occurrence in burned text promotes this into the same check.
