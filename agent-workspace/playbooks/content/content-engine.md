# Content Engine

**When to use:** planning or producing content of any kind — blog, social, email, video,
landing page copy.

---

## The rule

**Every piece of content maps to a funnel stage and a business objective.**

"For engagement" is not an objective. "For brand awareness" is not an objective unless
someone can say how it's measured and what it's worth. If a piece can't name what it's
for, it doesn't get made — that time goes to something that can.

| Stage | The reader's state | What content does here | Measured by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Doesn't know they have the problem | Name the problem in their language | Reach, new audience, branded search |
| **Consideration** | Comparing ways to solve it | Show how it's solved, and the trade-offs | Engagement, time on page, return visits |
| **Decision** | Choosing between options | Prove we're the right choice | Enquiries, bookings, conversion rate |
| **Retention** | Already a customer | Deepen the relationship, earn referrals | Repeat rate, referrals, churn |

Most agencies over-produce awareness content because it's easy and its failure is
invisible. Check the balance across a plan before committing to it.

## Planning

1. **Start from the audience, not the calendar.** What do they actually want to know?
   Sources: their own words in reviews and support tickets, sales-call objections, search
   queries, competitor comment sections.
2. **Map to objectives** from the client's `client-brief.md`.
3. **Pick channels by where the audience is**, not by where it's easy to post.
4. **Set a cadence that's sustainable** with the resources that actually exist. A
   consistent modest cadence outperforms an ambitious one that collapses in month two.
5. **Build the calendar** with owner, deadline and approver per piece.

## Producing

- Brief before writing — see [`../../templates/content-brief.md`](../../templates/content-brief.md)
- Write in the client's voice, from their `brand-guide.md`
- One idea per piece; a piece trying to do three jobs does none
- Lead with the value — earn the scroll in the first line
- Every piece has a next step, even if it's a small one
- Original substance: an opinion, a number, an example, a piece of experience. Content
  that restates what's already on the first page of results is invisible by design.
- Repurpose deliberately: one substantial piece should yield several derivative ones,
  planned at the outset rather than salvaged after.

## Reviewing

Before anything is published:

- ☐ On voice for whoever's name is on it
- ☐ Every claim and statistic sourced
- ☐ Proofread — slowly, once more
- ☐ Images licensed, alt text written
- ☐ Links work
- ☐ Client approval where required
- ☐ Meets the bar in [`../../operations/quality-bar.md`](../../operations/quality-bar.md)

## Measuring

Review monthly against what each piece was for:

- Which pieces did their job? Which didn't?
- What topics or formats consistently outperform?
- What can be updated rather than replaced? Refreshing a piece that already performs is
  usually a better return than another new one.
- Log the pattern in [`../../knowledge/learnings/`](../../knowledge/learnings/).

## Common failures

| Failure | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Content with no stated objective | The mapping rule — no purpose, no production |
| Publishing to a calendar nobody can sustain | Set cadence by real capacity |
| Everything at awareness stage | Check the funnel balance across the plan |
| Generic content indistinguishable from competitors | Require original substance in every brief |
| Agency voice bleeding into client content | Brand guide open while writing, every time |
| Nothing measured, so nothing learned | Monthly review is part of the engagement, not an extra |
