# Client Reporting

**When to use:** every recurring client report, and any one-off performance review.

**NEEDS INPUT:** standard cadence, format and tooling —
see [`../../company/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`](../../company/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).

---

## What a report is for

A client report answers three questions, in this order:

1. **What happened?** The numbers, against the targets we agreed.
2. **What does it mean?** Interpretation — the part the client is actually paying for.
3. **What are we doing about it?** The specific next actions, with owners and dates.

A dashboard screenshot answers only the first. Sending one without the other two is
handing the client a puzzle and calling it a deliverable.

## Structure

**1. Headline** — two or three sentences. Did we hit the objective? If a client read only
this, would they know where they stand? Write it last, once you know what the report says.

**2. Performance against targets**

| Metric | Target | Actual | vs. last period | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌ |

Report against the targets set at the campaign gate or in the client brief — not against
whichever metrics happened to look good.

**3. What we did** — briefly. The work, not a list of tasks.

**4. What we learned** — the insight from the period. This is what makes the report worth
reading and the retainer worth renewing.

**5. What's next** — specific actions, each with an owner and a date. Include what we
need from the client, and by when.

**6. Appendix** — the detailed data, for anyone who wants it.

## Rules

- **Every number is traceable.** Name the source platform and the export or file it came
  from, and state the date range. If a figure can't be traced, it doesn't go in.
- **Consistent date ranges.** Same window, same comparison, every period. Shifting the
  window to make a period look better is the fastest way to lose a client's trust
  permanently.
- **Lead with the business metric.** Enquiries, bookings, revenue — then the platform
  metrics that explain them.
- **Report bad periods plainly.** Say what happened, why, and what changes. Clients
  forgive a bad month; they don't forgive discovering it was buried.
- **No vanity metrics without context.** Impressions and reach mean something only when
  connected to an outcome.
- **Explain the jargon** or don't use it. The reader may not be a marketer.
- **Flag anomalies you can't explain** rather than smoothing them over. Tracking breaks;
  saying so early is much cheaper than being asked about it later.

## Before sending

- ☐ Every number checked against source
- ☐ Date ranges consistent with previous reports
- ☐ Targets are the ones actually agreed
- ☐ Interpretation written, not just data presented
- ☐ Next actions have owners and dates
- ☐ Proofread
- ☐ Meets [`../../operations/quality-bar.md`](../../operations/quality-bar.md)
- ☐ Saved to the client's `deliverables/` folder as `YYYY-MM-DD-report.md`

## After sending

Log anything notable in [`../../knowledge/learnings/`](../../knowledge/learnings/).
Reports are a monthly, structured look at what's working — the richest regular source of
learning the agency has, and the most routinely wasted.

## Common failures

| Failure | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Data with no interpretation | Sections 2 and 3 are mandatory |
| Metrics chosen after seeing the results | Report against pre-agreed targets |
| Date ranges shifting between reports | Fix the window at the start of the engagement |
| Bad news softened into invisibility | Report plainly; propose the fix |
| Numbers that can't be reproduced | Cite source and date range for every figure |
| Report sent, nothing learned from it | Log the learning the same day |
