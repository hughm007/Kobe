# Discovery Call

**When to use:** the first substantive conversation with a prospect, and again at the
start of any major new engagement with an existing client.

The goal is not to pitch. It's to understand the business well enough to know whether we
can help, and to price the work honestly. A proposal written from a thin discovery is a
proposal that gets underquoted, over-scoped, or both.

---

## Before the call

- Look at their website, their ads, their social, their reviews
- Note what you'd change and why — but hold it; opening with criticism costs you the
  honest answers you need
- Check who you're speaking to and whether they can actually decide

## The questions

Ask these; take notes verbatim where the language is good — their words are the raw
material for their copy later.

**The business**
1. Tell me about the business — what do you sell, and to whom?
2. How does someone become a customer today, start to finish?
3. What's a customer worth to you, roughly? How often do they come back?
4. What's your capacity — could you handle double the enquiries next month?

*That last one is the question most agencies skip. Selling volume to a business that
can't service it produces an unhappy client and a case study you can't use.*

**The problem**
5. What made you start looking for help now?
6. What have you tried already? What happened?
7. If we do nothing, what does the next 12 months look like?

**The customer**
8. Who are your best customers — and what do they have in common?
9. Why do people choose you over the alternatives?
10. Why do people choose someone else?
11. What do customers say when they're happy? (Ask for the actual words.)

**Success**
12. If this works, what's different in six months? Say it in numbers if you can.
13. How will you personally be judged on this?
14. What would make you consider it a failure?

**The practicals**
15. What budget have you set aside, and over what period?
16. Who else is involved in the decision?
17. Who signs off the work once we start?
18. Is there a deadline or event driving the timing?
19. What do you have already — brand assets, copy, photography, existing accounts?
20. Anything I should know that's gone wrong before?

## After the call

- Write the notes up **the same day**, into the client's `notes/` folder as
  `YYYY-MM-DD-discovery.md`. Detail decays fast.
- Fill as much of `client-brief.md` as the call supports; mark the gaps explicitly
- Decide honestly whether this is a fit — check against the anti-profile in
  [`../../company/positioning-and-icp.md`](../../company/positioning-and-icp.md)
- If it isn't a fit, say so. A declined bad-fit client costs one call; a signed one costs
  months and a reference you can't use.
- If it is, send the proposal within the timeframe you promised

## Common failures

| Failure | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Pitching instead of listening | The first ten minutes are questions only |
| Never asking about budget | Ask it plainly — vagueness protects nobody |
| Not knowing who decides | Ask directly, early |
| Missing that they can't service the volume | Question 4 |
| Notes written up three days later | Same day, always |
| Taking a bad-fit client because it's revenue | Check the anti-profile before proposing |
