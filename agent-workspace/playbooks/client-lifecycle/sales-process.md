# Sales

**The motion in one line: make the ad first. Send it with nothing attached. Let the work
start the conversation.** Service Pow has no public portfolio and no performance numbers
to cite — the spec ad IS the credibility argument. Show, then talk, then price.

## Cold email (primary channel)
Reply rates run under 1% — normal; the list quality and the spec ad carry it.
Subject names THEIR business, not our service · under 120 words · hosted link, never an
attachment · **"no charge, no catch — keep it either way"** said explicitly · one ask:
a 20-minute call · no AI talk. Cadence: day 0 the ad · day 4 "did this reach you?" ·
day 11 one different angle · day 18 the close-out (highest reply rate of the four) —
**then stop** and move to NURTURE with a revisit date.
LinkedIn lands the email (connect AFTER day 0, never before); where trades owners are
inactive there, IG/FB DM substitutes. One prospect, not two.

## Spec-ad discipline (what stops this becoming a treadmill)
EXPLORATION/TEST budget class only · batch in runs of 3–5 sharing research · angle
rotation applies to outreach too · every spec logged with angle and outcome ·
**hard stop: 10 spec ads → zero discovery calls = the targeting or offer is wrong; do
not send ten more.** And: 3 pilots → zero retainer conversions = fix the pilot.

## Discovery call (20–30 min, video — watch their face while they watch the ad)
2 min why they took the call (their words become the proposal) → 3 min watch the spec
together, silently → 10 min their business → **the two hard gates asked plainly**:
"can someone shoot ten seconds of phone video a few times a month from a list we send?"
and "would you give us ad-account access so we can see what's working?" → 5 min the
pilot, no discounting → agree a decision date. Never end with "let me know."

## Objections (the two that matter most)
**"This is AI, isn't it?"** — never deny it: *"Some of it. The parts that matter — your
trucks, your team, your logo — are real, and that's why it doesn't look like the AI
stuff you've seen. The point is whether it gets you calls."*
**"That's expensive."** — compare against alternatives, never our cost; reduce scope
before price.

## Proposal
One page, within 24 hours, opening with *what we heard* in their words. Floor or above,
scope explicit, usage rights quoted in. **Orion drafts; Karl sends. Always.**
Follow-up day 3, day 7, close-out.

## Pipeline mechanics
Stages: PROSPECT → CONTACTED → REPLIED → QUALIFIED → DISCOVERY → PROPOSAL → FOLLOW-UP →
VERBAL YES → CONTRACT → ONBOARDING → ACTIVE (or NURTURE with a mandatory revisit date /
LOST with a reason from the closed list: PRICE · TIMING · NO BUDGET · COMPETITOR ·
IN-HOUSE · NO RESPONSE · DISQUALIFIED BY US · BAD FIT IN DISCOVERY · WENT DARK).
**Every row carries owner, last contact, next action, next date — a row without them is
a wish, not a pipeline entry.** Weekly hygiene Mondays, 15 minutes.

**The capacity governor:** monthly delivery capacity is UNKNOWN (never measured);
working ceiling **4 finished ads/month across all clients**, so **no more than 2 deals
at PROPOSAL-or-beyond at once** until a real month is measured. A pipeline larger than
capacity is a promise-breaking machine.

**List construction:** ONE vertical × ONE metro, 60–100 named businesses, researched —
start with Phoenix metro (911 Drain context already exists), then rotate. Per prospect
before first touch: owner name, current advertising (Meta Ad Library, LSA, reviews),
the specific angle, footage likelihood.

**Who does what:** Orion/Claude owns list research, angles, spec production drafting,
outreach copy, pipeline hygiene and loss analysis. Karl owns qualification, pricing,
proposals, closes, discovery calls. Hugh owns outreach execution and his network.
**The AI never sends, never prices, never promises. That line does not move.**
