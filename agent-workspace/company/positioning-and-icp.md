# Positioning

**Home services and local trades are the WEDGE, not the ceiling.** Trades first because
that's where the proof is fastest: the realism playbook was developed on trades footage,
owners are directly reachable, one recovered customer pays for a month of retainer, and
real footage is abundant.

**How to say it:** *"We're a marketing company — video, email, ads, the whole thing.
Right now most of our production work is with home-service businesses, because that's
where we've built the deepest playbook."* Never "we only work with plumbers." Never
"we work with anyone" either.

**Differentiators that are actually defensible:**
1. The spec ad made for *their* business before they've asked — cost structure makes
   this affordable as a first cold touch; nobody else's does.
2. Realism discipline (real logos, real references, hybrid real+AI) — the answer to
   "it looks AI," which research shows consumers punish.
3. **"Platform-safe AI ads"** — disclosure handled, no fake testimonials, claims
   substantiated. Cheap competitors ignore this; it removes a risk the client didn't
   know they had.
4. Tested variants, not single guesses — "you're getting four tested variants, not one."

# ICP and qualification

**Hard gates — all must be YES before a proposal:**
- [ ] Decision-maker access (for trades, nearly always the owner)
- [ ] Ability to pay the pilot without visible strain
- [ ] A real marketing problem, stated by them — not diagnosed by us and sold back
- [ ] **Will send footage monthly** (a "no" means pure-AI: weakest product, +25% price, flagged)
- [ ] No regulated-claim minefield we're not equipped for (healthcare, finance,
  insurance, legal, cannabis, credit → escalate, never wing it)

**Automatic disqualifiers:** wants guaranteed lead counts · pay-on-results ·
unlimited-revision expectations · no camera AND no footage but wants photoreal people ·
haggles the pilot price before discovery.

**Soft fit signals:** responds professionally, has spent on marketing before, has an
offer worth advertising, reviews suggest they actually deliver — *we cannot make a bad
business look good, and trying damages our reputation, not just theirs.*

**Adjacent-vertical rule:** a non-trades prospect is never turned away — qualify on the
same criteria, take it if it passes, log it as ADJACENT so we see when a second vertical
earns its own playbook.
