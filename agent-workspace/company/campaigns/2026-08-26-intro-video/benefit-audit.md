# Benefit audit — does this film say what changes for the customer?

**Why this exists.** Owner brief, 2026-08-30: *"think about how the video shows Service Pow's
business and how they benefit customers."* Judged against that question rather than "is it
finished", the film has one mechanical problem. This file measures it.

**The finding in one line:** the film is a **capability list, not a benefit story**. It says what
Service Pow makes. It never says what changes for the person watching.

---

## 1. The muted read-out — the only message most viewers get

`playbooks/ads/video-production.md` step 4b:

> **Lock the placement's SOUND ASSUMPTION (v3.1):** homepage heroes and most feed placements
> autoplay MUTED — the story must land with zero audio (visual hook, on-screen text beats,
> readable action); the audio bed is a bonus layer for the sound-on minority.

So the eighteen burned strings **are** the film. In order, that is:

> One ad. One guess. → SERVICE POW → video ads · websites · social → 2 concepts × 2 hooks →
> 4 finished ads → 5–7 days from your footage → Your footage stays the hero → AI builds the rest →
> Disclosure handled · No fake testimonials · Claims substantiated → The page it lands on →
> Captions. End cards. Titles. → We make the first one before you ask. → servicepow.com

Read that as a plumber who has never heard of us. It is a **list of things a company makes**. Not
one string tells him what is different in his week, his month or his phone log afterwards.

**This corrects something told to the Owner earlier in the session.** The silent cut was described
as a limitation. It is not — muted is the *design target* for a homepage hero, and audio is a bonus
layer. That makes the burned text more load-bearing, not less.

## 2. The you-to-we ratio — measured

| | control |
|---|---|
| total VO words | 124 |
| you / your | **4** |
| we / our / Service Pow | **4** |
| ratio | **4 : 4** |

A benefit-led film should run roughly **3 : 1** toward "you". But the ratio understates the problem.
Look at where the four "you"s actually land:

| "you" | Attached to |
|---|---|
| "**you** don't get one video" | a deliverable |
| "**your** footage stays the hero" | a method |
| "for **your** business" | the CTA |
| "before **you** ask" | the CTA |

**Not one "you" is attached to an outcome.** The customer appears as a recipient of deliverables and
never as someone whose situation improves.

## 3. Benefit connectives — zero

Searched every VO line and every burned string for the words that complete a benefit sentence:
*so you · which means · instead of · you find out · you stop · no more · without*.

**Zero hits.** The only two matches anywhere in `script.md` are inside prose commentary the viewer
never sees. There is no sentence in this film that finishes the thought.

## 4. The feature→benefit table

| Beat | What it says | What the customer actually gets | Said? | The sentence it should have said |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HOOK | "Most marketing money buys one ad… no way to know what worked" | *(states a market condition; costs him nothing to hear)* | — | **"You paid for one ad. It didn't work. And you still don't know why."** |
| WHO | "Service Pow makes video ads. We also build the sites and run the social around them." | one company, so the ad and the page stop contradicting each other | **no** | **"Service Pow makes your ads, your site and your social — so they all say the same thing."** |
| PACKS | "four finished ads in five to seven business days" | he finds out which message works instead of betting everything on one | **no** | **"So you get four, not one — so you find out which one works instead of betting on one."** |
| REAL+AI | "your footage stays the hero" | it doesn't get skipped for looking fake — `positioning-and-icp.md`: *"the answer to 'it looks AI,' which research shows consumers punish"* | **no** | **"Your own footage stays the hero, so it doesn't get scrolled past for looking fake."** |
| SAFE | "disclosure handled, no fake testimonials, every claim backed up" | his ad account doesn't get pulled | **no** | **"So your ad account doesn't get pulled."** |
| BUILD | "we build the page, the captions, the end cards — in code" | the clicks he already paid for land somewhere that converts | **no** | **"So you stop paying to send people somewhere that loses them."** |
| START | "we'll make one for your business — before you ask" | he sees the work before he spends | **partly** | *(SIGNED 2026-08-28 — kept verbatim, not rewritten)* |
| END | wordmark + URL | where to go | n/a | — |

**Six of eight beats state a feature and stop.** In every case the benefit is one clause away and
the film never spends it.

## 5. Feeling Spec — the control, honestly assessed

Format per `servicepow-storyboard-director/references/shot-fields.md`; worked example at
`clients/911drain/campaigns/2026-08-26-storyboard-rebuild/shotlist.md` §Feeling Spec.
**This artifact was never written for this film — that omission is the direct cause of §4.** You
cannot leave a beat feeling-less if you are forced to name its feeling and its observable cause.

| Beat | Feeling (specific) | Observable on-screen cause |
|---|---|---|
| HOOK | mild recognition | a lone sketched ad and a question mark — **nothing is at stake** |
| WHO | orientation — *information, not feeling* | the wordmark and three icons appear |
| PACKS | interest | one sketch becomes four finished ads in two frames |
| REAL+AI | reassurance | the plate does not change while everything around it snaps |
| SAFE | **none** — the beat informs, it does not make the viewer feel anything | three ticks land on a list |
| BUILD | **none** — breadth is a fact, not a feeling | a wireframe fills in |
| START | mild curiosity | a van and a shopfront snap finished |
| END | resolve | the wordmark settles |

**Two of eight beats produce no feeling at all**, and they are the two that together occupy
**16 of 60 seconds (27%)**, in the back half where attention is thinnest.

## 6. Sound Spine — the control

Trivially short, because there is no audio: **silence throughout, all sixty seconds.** Per §1 that
is the correct target and not a defect. Recorded here only so the artifact exists and so the
challenger's version has something to be compared against.

## 7. The stakes problem

The hook says a buyer has "no way to know what worked." That is *mildly annoying*. It never says
what it costs, and the cost is the whole reason to keep watching.

What it actually costs a local business owner: **the money is gone and he learned nothing**, so the
next decision is another guess. Not "he wasted $800" — that he could absorb. The damage is that a
year of spending leaves him exactly as ignorant as he started, still unable to tell whether the ad
was wrong, the offer was wrong, or the timing was wrong.

**No invented statistic is needed to say that**, and none will be used. The stakes come from the
situation, not from a figure.

## 8. The missing customer

Zero people appear in this film. It shows artifacts — ad frames, a grid, a browser window, a van —
and never the person who owns them.

**Owner ruling, 2026-08-30: put a drawn customer in, at the hook and again at the payoff.** In the
existing marker vocabulary, since nothing photographic may be fabricated. A clumsy figure is worse
than no figure, so it is **built and shown as a still before any full render commits to it**; if it
fails that checkpoint the challenger proceeds artifact-only and the reason is recorded here.

## 9. Conclusion

The control is well made and says the wrong things. The production discipline is not the problem —
it is, again, stronger than the advertising copy, which is the same note the Owner gave on the
911 Drain board. The fix is not more craft. It is **six clauses**, a hook with a cost in it, and a
person on screen to spend them on.

Feeds directly into `challenger-board.md`.

## 10. Independent adversarial read — what a cold reader found that this file missed

Run in an isolated context with **no production reasoning**, the same way the Skeptic is run. Its
findings are recorded here whether or not they agree with §§1–9. Six survived my verification; each
is marked with what I checked.

### 10a. The viewer is grammatically passive for the entire film — measured

| | count in the 124 VO words |
|---|---|
| Active verbs whose subject is **Service Pow** | **7** — makes · build · run · build · make · handled · backed up |
| Active verbs whose subject is **the viewer** | **2** — "don't get", "ask" |

And both of the viewer's two verbs are *receptive*. He never does anything, decides anything or
gains anything. This is a sharper statement of §2: sixty seconds of a vendor doing things near a
customer. **Verified by reading every line; the counts are exact.**

### 10b. The hook is a broken promise inside the film's own logic

The hook asks *how do I know what worked?* Beats 2–6 then answer four **different** questions: how
much do I get · does it look fake · is it legal · where does it point. **Nothing in the film ever
answers the question it opens with.** This is a structural defect and §§1–9 missed it entirely.

### 10c. The SAFE beat says the same thing twice and the benefit neither time

The picture ticks *disclosure handled · no fake testimonials · claims substantiated* while the VO
reads the same three items aloud. Two channels, one message, and the consequence stated in neither.
Handing the list to the picture and the consequence to the voice buys back roughly **two seconds
for free**. **Verified against `script.md` §2 and `storyboard.md` S7 — the duplication is exact.**

### 10d. "Your part is small" is the biggest missing benefit — and it is sourced

`company/services.md` line 26, verbatim:

> **Hybrid real+AI is the default promise:** client's real **phone** footage as hero shots, AI
> environments and B-roll.

The word **phone** is in the source and the film never says it. The film says "your footage", which
sounds like *a production*. The truth is smaller and far more relieving: **clips off your phone,
between jobs — nobody comes to your site.** That is the single most persuasive fact in the offer and
it is absent. **VERIFIED — quoted directly from the services file.**

### 10e. The BUILD beat implies a website is included. It is not. — CLAIM DEFECT

| Check | Finding |
|---|---|
| Is the page part of the pilot pack? | **No.** `services.md`: *"Pilot pack (the front door): 2 concepts × 2 hooks = 4 finished ads, one strategy call, 5–7 business days"* — no page |
| Where do websites live? | `services.md` **§4**, a separate service line |
| Are they priced? | **No.** `pricing-and-packaging.md` has `Pilot pack $1,200` and `Single one-off ad $400` — **no website line exists** |

"The ad needs somewhere to land. **We build the page**…" arrives twenty seconds after the pack is
described, and a viewer reasonably concludes the page comes with it. It does not, and it has no
price. **This is the same defect class as the 5–7 day conditional caught on 2026-08-28 — second
instance.** Captions and end cards are fine (the pack ships *"captioned, platform-ready"*); the page
is not.

### 10f. The grey plate is a consequence of having no person — and there is a fix

At the exact second the VO says *your footage stays the hero*, the screen shows an empty grey
rectangle. **The proudest claim in the film is illustrated by an absence** — a viewer who doesn't
read the label sees an asset that failed to load.

The cold read's solve, which is better than the one in §8: **the placeholder goes inside a drawn
phone.** A line-art hand holds a phone in the hook — screen dark, no notification, giving "the phone
didn't ring" a body to land on. At the footage beat the *same hand holds the same phone up, filming*,
and the plate lives inside its frame, filling with the jobsite and snapping to finished.

**The phone that stayed quiet becomes the phone that does the work.** It carries almost no realism
risk — it is a hand and a rectangle, no face, no identifiable person, nothing that could read as a
testimonial. Adopted for the challenger.

### 10g. The budget is five to one against the only uncopyable thing

Breadth (in WHO) ≈ 5s, plus SAFE and BUILD running 36.9→52.2 = 15.3s. **≈20 of 60 seconds on what
Service Pow does and how it conducts itself. The spec-ad offer — the one thing a competitor cannot
say — gets 4.2 seconds and no instruction.**

The end card then points at `servicepow.com`, which is **the page the viewer is already standing on**.

  Replaced with **"keep the one that pulls"**, which describes how a test resolves rather than
