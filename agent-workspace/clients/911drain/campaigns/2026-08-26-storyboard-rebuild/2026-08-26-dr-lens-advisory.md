## ADVISORY — CONTROL cut (trust-first, Revision 14)

**1. Time-to-value-proposition.** The problem is on screen at 0.0s (A: water rising; B: rim overflow) — DR-correct self-selection. The problem being *solved* — the thing a mid-emergency cold viewer is actually buying — first appears at **9.0s** (S4, cable into the drain), 53% of runtime elapsed. What sits in front of it: 3.5s of performed verification (S2) and 3.0s of silent dusk arrival (S3) — 6.5 seconds of trust-craft between problem and fix. This is not an accident; it is the cut's thesis. Mitigations already on the board: a callable number from 5.5s (strap onset), and the proposition the ad *chooses* to declare by 3s is the trust proposition ("You can check your plumber's license yourself"), not a fix proposition — internally consistent with the hypothesis, and exactly the structural ceiling the Owner's read #2 named. Variant-C nuance: for C's audience the value proposition *is* the lookup, delivered from 0.2s — and C is correctly barred from cold in the media plan, so this never faces a cold viewer.

**2. CTA directness.** The close **asks**: "Call 911 Drain — the number's on your screen." — verb first, brand named, action named; number set from frame one; unvoiced digits carried by a 3.0s on-screen dwell (a sound design choice, not an evasion — the VO points the eye at the number). One observation: the endcard carries a second imperative ("— look us up."), so the close holds two verbs. In this cut that is the concept's payoff (C's loop deliberately resolves there), so it is coherent rather than diluting — noted, not flagged. Constraint ceiling: the close cannot lawfully escalate to urgency/availability ("now", "24/7") until the claims sheet signs; the board already records this as the commercial bottleneck and the ending "quieter than the feed" is the trust aesthetic doing what the claims discipline would force anyway. The ceiling here is the constraint plus the chosen posture, not sloppy copy.

**3. Persuasion vs. cleverness.** Cleverest element: the **performed verification ritual** — the C1→S2 continuous take typing the real license number into the real registrar page, in camera. Runner-up: the S3 wordless arrival ("the wait is the shot"). The removal thought-experiment: strip the ritual to its evidentiary core and reassign S3's seconds to solution/CTA and you get, almost line for line, the challenger board — the experiment is not hypothetical, it has been built. Honest paper answer to the read's question: for the cold mid-emergency A/B audiences, a stranger is *more* likely to act with those seconds on the fix; for the distrust-primed viewer the control is betting the reverse — that watching verification performed is what converts at all. Neither answer is decidable on paper, and the pack correctly refuses to decide it there.

**4. Challenger exists.** **Satisfied by construction.** The challenger board exists, Owner-directed, built entirely from the control's captures, with the metric chain stated before launch and the S3 beat tested by cutting rather than argument — the doctrine's own mechanism, executed to the letter. The rule's exemption clause is not needed.
