## 2. How Instagram decides who sees a post (the four ranking systems)

Instagram doesn't show posts in order. Each surface ranks with its own system, predicting who
will watch, like, share or save.

| Surface | Mostly shows to | What it rewards | How TripNerd uses it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feed** | Followers | Relationships: people who comment, DM and reply see you first | Reply to every comment; proof posts and carousels worth saving |
| **Stories** | Your closest followers | People who watch and reply to your Stories repeatedly | Stories 5 days a week with polls, quizzes, questions, behind-the-scenes and DM prompts |
| **Explore** | People who don't follow you | Momentum: how many like, comment, share and save, and how fast | Saveable guides, and a Story that pushes each new post within its first hour |
| **Reels** | Mostly people who don't follow you | Reels watched to the end, shared, and whose audio gets used | A hook in the first second, real footage, original sound, a reason to send it |

Source: Instagram, "Shedding more light on how Instagram works" (2021) and "Instagram Ranking
Explained" (2023), as summarized in TripNerd's approved growth plan. OFFICIAL.

### The three signals that matter most right now

1. **Watch time:** whether people keep watching past the first seconds and finish. The
   biggest signal across surfaces.
2. **Sends per reach:** how often people DM the post to someone. The strongest signal for
   reaching people who don't follow you yet.
3. **Likes per reach:** still counts, mostly with existing followers.

Source: Adam Mosseri (head of Instagram), as widely reported for 2025–26, e.g.
[eclincher 2026 summary](https://www.eclincher.com/articles/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works-in-2026).
The ranking is OFFICIAL; the summary is THIRD-PARTY. **Grok: confirm against Mosseri's own
posts and @creators before relying on it.** Some articles say sends are weighted "3–5×
likes"; that figure is unverified, so don't repeat it.

### Recent changes to know (2026)

- **Originality (April 2026):** accounts that mostly post reposted content are classed as
  "aggregators" and get less reach across Reels, Explore and Feed. Near-duplicates and
  watermarked videos (e.g. a TikTok logo) are demoted. What counts is whether you added
  something material. THIRD-PARTY reporting:
  [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/),
  [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/es/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm).
  → **TripNerd only posts its own footage and edits. Never repost, never post with another
  app's watermark.**
- **Trial Reels:** a Reel can be shown to non-followers first, so a hook gets tested without
  risking the main feed. Trial Reels count toward the plan's Reel total. → **Use them to
  test 2–3 different first seconds of the same video.**

## 3. How we beat each signal (the tactics)

| Signal | What makes it go up | TripNerd rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Watch time** | A hook in the first second; one idea; a payoff saved for the end; quick cuts; 20–35 seconds; burned-in captions; a loop where the last frame matches the first | Open on the most surprising thing ("This sandwich has cost $1.50 since 2002"), not the logo. Rankings and countdowns hold people to the end. |
| **Sends** | Content made for one specific person: the golf group chat, the friend who always plans the trip, the boss who hosts clients | Every Reel answers "who would I send this to?" before it's made. Insider facts, price shocks and arguments get sent. |
| **Saves** | Something useful later: cheat sheets, checklists, "what to know before you go" | Carousels and Reels with "Nerd Notes" lists for each event |
| **Follows** | A series people want more of; a clear niche | Named recurring series ("Nerd Notes," "Rating the menu," "Guess the event"), always sports/events/hospitality |
| **Comments** | A question people want to answer, or a ranking they disagree with | End with a choice ("Which are you ordering first?") or a hot take ("Egg salad beats pimento cheese") |
| **Explore momentum** | Fast early engagement | Post at the plan's times, push each new post in Stories within the hour, reply to comments in the first hour |
| **Originality** | Your own footage, transformed edits | Real TripNerd footage and photos, real people, original voice |

**Stories mechanics (sourced, THIRD-PARTY benchmarks):**

- **Length:** keep sets to 3–6 frames. Retention stays above 75% for about 6–7 frames a day
  ([Rival IQ](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/instagram-stories-benchmark-report/)).
- **Frame 1 does the work:** it has the highest exit rate, 23.8%
  ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-stories-benchmarks)).
- **Safe zones:** keep text out of the top 14% and bottom 20% of a 1080×1920 frame (Meta
  guidance).
- **Captions:** Meta found captions raised average view duration by 12%. Instagram says
  about 60% of Stories video is watched with sound on, so design for both.
- **Stickers:** polls, quizzes and countdowns are low-effort for viewers to answer. Question
  boxes and Add Yours stickers do poorly on small accounts.
- **Link stickers** work for every account. Use links with tracking tags.

**Posting schedule (from TripNerd's growth plan, Eastern Time):**

| Format | When |
|---|---|
| Stories | Mon–Fri, 10 AM |
| Reels | Mon 2 PM; Fri 11 AM (Trial Reel). From Day 31, also Tue 6 PM and Wed 12 PM |
| Carousel | Tue 1 PM |
| Proof post | Thu 12:30 PM |

Plus a Story pushing each new post within the hour. Tune these to Instagram Insights' "most
active times" once there's data.

## 4. The creative strategy: sneak into what people already watch

Some people binge food reviews, some golf, some price breakdowns. **Borrow the format the
crowd loves and put TripNerd's world inside it.** The format comes from the crowd; the
subject is always sports, events, hospitality or travel, so the people we attract are fans
and hosts, not people who'll never care about events.

| Popular format | TripNerd version | Footage needed |
|---|---|---|
| Food review / taste test | **"The $1.50 Augusta menu, made at home (and ranked)"** (script ready); stadium food vs suite food at events from Feb | Phone + kitchen |
| "Rating X 1–10" | Rating hospitality spreads from TripNerd's archive photos, with a Nerd's real voiceover | Archive photos |
| Price breakdown ("what $X gets you") | "What a Derby weekend actually costs" (TripNerd confirms the ranges) | Graphics + talking head |
| "Things that make zero sense" | Augusta: phones banned so there are payphones, $1.50 sandwiches | Talking head + photos |
| Guess the sound / quiz | "Guess the event from the crowd," using roars from TripNerd's own footage | Existing audio |
| POV skit | "POV: a client wants 12 rooms in Augusta in April" | Staff, phone |
| Day in the life | A Nerd during event week | Staff, phone |
| Tier list / ranking | Event traditions: Derby hats, Phoenix 16th, the island green at Sawgrass | Archive + talking head |
| Creator collab | A food or golf creator at a real event (allowed from Day 31) | The creator films |

Benchmark from the plan: **Golfbreaks' creator Reel did 7.4× its median, and On Location's
people-on-camera Reels did 10× its brand graphics.** People on camera beat graphics.

**The "Nerd" device:** every video should teach, reveal or rank something a fan didn't know.
Never reuse the old feed slogans: "knowing the right people", "bucket list → booked",
"details handled", "skip the client dinner", "memories last".

## 5. The food-review idea: what's allowed and what isn't

**Not allowed:** an AI-generated person "at the TripNerd suite" saying "here's what TripNerd
offered us today… this was the best."

- It's a fake customer review. The FTC Fake Reviews Rule bans it, and an AI label doesn't
  fix it.
- TripNerd's approved plan says "AI never creates a person, a voice or a testimonial."
- AI-generated food shown as what TripNerd served misrepresents the product.

**Allowed, and better:**

- **A real Nerd (TripNerd staff) tastes and rates real food:** at home (the Augusta menu
  recreation) or at a real event.
- **Real archive photos with a real person's voiceover.**
- **A real creator at a real event.**
- **AI only as a short, labelled bridge between real shots, or to upscale.** Never for
  people, faces or voices.

**No footage? Fill the gap with:** a real Nerd on camera, an at-home recreation, photo edits
with a real voiceover, screen-recording explainers, or labelled AI bridges between real
shots. Never fake people.

## 6. Hard rules (TripNerd-approved; never break, and flag any idea that would)

- AI never creates a person, face, voice or testimonial. AI is only for short bridges
  between real shots and upscaling, always labelled as AI.
- Reviews and guest quotes are real and word for word. Identifiable guests only with
  consent (a photo release at check-in).
- Event names only to say where guests went ("our guests at THE PLAYERS"). Never imply
  official partnership. No league or tournament logos, no broadcast footage.
- Follow venue filming rules. Augusta bans phones on the grounds, so there's no footage from
  inside.
- Any number, price or "best/most" claim needs a source. TripNerd prices and inclusions need
  TripNerd's confirmation.
- Music: original audio or Instagram's commercial-use library only. No trending copyrighted
  tracks on the business account.
- Nothing posts without TripNerd's approver signing off the weekly batch.

## 8. What Grok can and can't do here

- **Can:** search the web and X live (DeepSearch), read uploaded files (Insights exports,
  this brief), write hooks, scripts and shot lists, and generate visuals (Grok Imagine,
  within the AI rule only).
- **Can't:** log into Instagram, see TripNerd's Insights unless they're pasted in, or see
  private view data for other accounts. Instagram doesn't publish watch data by industry.
- **So:** "what food content gets" comes from public view counts and third-party
  benchmarks. Treat it as directional and say so.

**How Grok must report research:**

- Cite every claim with a source and date.
- Label it OFFICIAL (Instagram, Meta, Mosseri, @creators), THIRD-PARTY (Later, Sprout,
  Socialinsider, Hootsuite, etc.), OBSERVED (posts or accounts actually opened) or
  INFERENCE.
- Flag anything older than 6 months.
- Never invent statistics, view counts, accounts or links. Write "unverified" instead.

**Output format for every Reel idea:**

> Title · format borrowed (from what kind of account) · why people watch to the end · who
> they'd send it to and why · hook (first-second visual + on-screen text + first spoken
> line) · shot list with the source of every shot (REAL ASSET / NEW PHONE FOOTAGE / AI
> BRIDGE, labelled) · voiceover script · on-screen captions · CTA (comment prompt or DM
> keyword) · the main algorithm signal it targets · rule check.

## 9. Prompts to run

**A. Monthly algorithm briefing**
> Using live web and X search, brief me on how Instagram currently ranks Reels, Explore, Feed
> and Stories. Prioritize what Instagram, Adam Mosseri and @creators said in the last 6
> months. Table: signal | surface | what was said | source + date | OFFICIAL or THIRD-PARTY.
> Then list what changed in the last 90 days, and 5 implications for a 4,700-follower brand
> account trying to reach non-followers. No percentages you can't source. Update section 2
> of my briefing file with anything that changed.

**B. Niche format scan ("what everyone watches")**
> Search X and the web for short-form formats getting outsized views right now in: food
> reviews and stadium food, golf, horse racing / Kentucky Derby, NFL fan culture, NASCAR,
> luxury travel, and corporate hospitality. For each niche give 3–5 recurring formats with 2
> real example posts or accounts (with links), what makes them rewatchable and sendable, and
> whether each is OBSERVED or INFERRED. Flag anything you couldn't open.

**C. Sneak-in generator**
> For each format from the scan, write 2 TripNerd versions using only our assets and hard
> rules, in the standard output format. Rank all ideas by (likely sends + watch time) ÷
> production effort, and mark which can be made this week with no new footage.

**D. Script a Reel**
> Turn idea #__ into a 20–35 second Reel: a second-by-second beat sheet, on-screen text,
> voiceover, the source of every shot, a caption whose first line is the hook, 3 comment
> prompts, and 3 alternative first seconds to test as Trial Reels. Follow the hard rules.

**E. Weekly Insights review**
> Here is last week's Instagram Insights export: [paste]. Rank posts by sends per reach,
> saves per reach, average watch time, follows and views from non-followers. What patterns
> win? What do we stop? Give 3 tests for next week, each as a hypothesis with the one metric
> that decides it.

**F. Pre-post check (run on every script)**
> Check this script against my hard rules and the six algorithm signals. List every claim
> that needs a source, anything that could read as a fake review or official partnership,
> any logo or footage-rights risk, and the weakest second of the video. Suggest one fix for
> each.
