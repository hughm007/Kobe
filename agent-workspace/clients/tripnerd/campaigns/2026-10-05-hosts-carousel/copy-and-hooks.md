## 1. Mini hook tournament (cover, card c1)

**How it was scored:** 1–5 on each axis, out of 25. On the risk axis, 5 means low risk.

| Axis | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Stop** | Would a corporate host stop scrolling? |
| **Topic** | Is "golf" plain on frame 1, with words people search? See [`algorithm-niche-strategy.md`](../2026-10-instagram-growth/algorithm-niche-strategy.md) §4. |
| **Clarity** | Is it understood in one read? |
| **Fit** | Does it set up the four-moment body? |
| **Risk** | Claims, tone and engagement-bait risk. |

| # | Cover hook | Stop | Topic | Clarity | Fit | Risk | Total | Expected failure mode | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | "Hosting clients at a golf tournament?" | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | **25** | Narrows reach to people who host. That's intended: the buyer is the corporate host | **Lead** |
| **H2** | "What hosts get wrong at golf tournaments." | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 | **22** | Tells the buyer they're doing it wrong; "get wrong" lists can read as bait | **Alternate** (re-cover if H1 under-reaches) |
| H3 | "The 17th is the easy part." | 4 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 17 | Only lands with people who know 17 is THE PLAYERS' island green. A corporate host may not decode it | Beaten (clarity) |
| H4 | "Four moments decide a hosted golf day." | 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 22 | A statement with no tension. It works better as the support line, which is where it went | Beaten (kept as the support line) |
| H5 | "Your clients will remember four things." | 4 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 16 | No golf on frame 1; a prediction about memory we can't support | Beaten (topic, claim) |
| H6 | "The suite is booked. Now what?" | 4 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 18 | Assumes a suite; no golf word | Beaten (topic) |
| H7 | "Corporate hosts: the golf-day checklist." | 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 22 | Labels the format but gives no reason to stop | Beaten (stop power) |
| H8 | "Don’t let lunch cost your guests the big moment." | 4 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 5 | 18 | Covers one moment of four | Beaten (fit; it's card c3's job) |
| H9 | "Hosting at a tournament is four decisions." | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 21 | Flat | Beaten |
| H10 | "Before you host clients at a golf event, read this." | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 18 | "Read this" is an explicit instruction, the engagement-bait pattern the strategy warns against | Beaten (risk) |
| H11 | "Where will your guests eat when 17 erupts?" | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 17 | One moment only; assumes the 17th | Beaten (fit) |

**Pick:** H1 leads. H2 is the alternate.
- H1 scores higher because it names the exact situation and carries the search words. It also opens without telling the buyer they're wrong.
- Carousels can't run as Trial Reels. So H2 is held for a re-cover if H1's non-follower reach is weak. That's for Karl to decide after week-one Insights.

## 2. The sequence (one ad, seven cards)

| Card | Job | What the viewer knows after |
|---|---|---|
| c1 | Hook: names the situation and promises four moments | "This is for me, and there are four things" |
| c2–c5 | One moment each: the mistake ("Don’t…"), the fix, and TripNerd's own proof where a record supports it | What to plan for arrival, timing, dining and the exit |
| c6 | The save card: the four as questions | A checklist to keep |
| c7 | Payoff and CTA: TripNerd does this | Where to go: tripnerd.com |

**Word budgets** (layout law: headline ≤ 7, support ≤ 14, CTA ≤ 4): all within.
- Headlines: 6, 7, 7, 6, 7, 6 and 4 words.
- Supports: at most 12 words.
- CTAs: at most 4 words; the "→" isn't counted.

### Changes from the brief's copy v1, and why
1. **c4 proof:** "the food was steps from the rail" became "**the buffet** was steps from the rail".
   - The record's own word is "buffet counter" (V23, 16–19 s). Same meaning, exact to the evidence.
   - "Steps" stays, labelled INFERRED: the camera reaches the rail about 5.5 s after leaving the buffet, in one take.
   - Fallback if the gate rejects "steps": "In our suite on 17, the buffet and the rail were one short walk apart." (EV-tripnerd-003).
2. **c6 labels added:** the kicker "YOUR CHECKLIST", and the moment labels "1 · ARRIVAL" … "4 · THE EXIT" above each question.
   - These are structural labels repeated from cards c2–c5, so the checklist maps back to the four moments.
   - They add no claim.
3. **Big numerals "1"–"4" on c2–c5:** graphic elements set as real type, per the design brief.
4. **Apostrophes set typographically:** Don’t, When’s, Where’s.

No other wording changed. No claim was added.
