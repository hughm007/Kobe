## 2. How a Reel travels, and what ours are built to trigger

The ranking statements are Instagram's, relayed through secondary sources because instagram.com is not reachable from this session (sources at the end). Everything in the two right-hand columns is **design intent (HYPOTHESIS)** until week 1 Insights say otherwise. There is no TripNerd performance data yet; the plan says so too.

| Signal | What Instagram has said (status) | Reel 01 earns it by | Reel 03 earns it by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Watch time, completion, replays** | The most weighted signal for Reels; the first seconds decide whether a Reel gets a wider test audience (FACT: Mosseri, 2024, via secondary sources) | 17 s. A clock that keeps changing is an open loop ("what's at 6 PM?"); a fast-forward beat at 5 s breaks the rhythm; the last frame returns the clock to 6:40 AM on the first photo, so the loop is seamless and replays count as views | 16 s. The hook promises two ways and withholds the roar until 10.6 s; the last 0.2 s cuts back to the crowd walk for a seamless loop |

A day plan for a group is a thing people send to the group (HYPOTHESIS) | "Send this to whoever you'd bring." The two-ways contrast is an argument people forward to the friend who is "fine on the couch" (HYPOTHESIS) |
| **Likes per reach** | Matters more with people who already follow (FACT, same source) | Posted to followers at the plan's slot, with a Story push within the hour | Trials are not shown to followers; the winner is shared to followers Monday |
| **Saves** | An engagement signal, and the lever the plan assigns to Explore (FACT: Instagram's 2021 and 2023 ranking explainers) | A by-the-clock plan is saveable by anyone planning April 2027; the caption says "save it for April" | Lower by design; this one is for sends |
